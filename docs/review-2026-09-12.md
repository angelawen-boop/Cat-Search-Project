Reading these notes: a heading marked **COUNT CORRECT** means she has verified the

## Her overall verdict across the 12

- **Dates and titles — generally good.**
- **Not pulling in non-exhibitions — generally good.**
- **Pagination — isolated misses on a few sites**, listed per venue below, PLUS one
  general concern (next bullet).
- **Year-archive addresses, her general concern:** where a venue changes year with a
  drop-down that changes the page address, she believed the recipes wire in 2025 and
  2024 by hand and would silently stop covering 2026, 2027 and onward.
  **Checked 12 Sep: already solved, no work needed.** No recipe contains a year.
  `expandYearArchive()` derives them at run time from the lookback floor's year up to
  last year, so the set widens on its own as time passes. Fixtures Y-001 to Y-005
  hold the line. Reported back to her.
- **Biggest issue: junk text in the summaries.** Picture captions, "join us on X for
  the opening talk", ticketing information. Two distinct harms, and the second is the
  serious one:
  1. Noise the compression model should be able to ignore.
  2. Text the model **cannot tell apart from the blurb** — an artist's name in a photo
     caption reads exactly like an artist named in the exhibition text, so compression
     can state something false with no way to know.
- **Some summaries are ticketing information and nothing else.**

**The nine extras are all Displays.** The V&A opens every card with its own label
— `Exhibition`, `Upcoming Exhibition`, `Display`. South Kensington carries 2 + 4 +
9 of them, and her 2 and 4 are exactly the first two labels. They are now dropped
on the venue's own tag, anchored to the start of the card because one title ends
"On Display".

**The summaries were not junk mixed into good text — there was no good text to
find.** A V&A exhibition page carries NO curatorial paragraph at all. Every
substantial `<p>` on it belongs to the newsletter form, a membership promo or the
cookie-consent panel, and the real blurb sits in a `div` the site marks
`qa-exhibition-description`. The shared extraction ladder expects paragraphs at
every rung, so it fell through to the bottom and stored marketing on all 15 rows.

Two changes, one per-venue and one engine-wide:

- **A venue may now name its own description container in its recipe**, tried
  before the shared ladder and read whatever its tag. Where the blurb sits is the
  same kind of per-venue fact as where the title and dates sit.
- **The noise list is split in two.** The consent panel WAS recognised and then
  let through, because the guard written for the Wallace Collection says a
  container holding most of the page's prose is a layout wrapper rather than a
  banner. On a page with no real prose the OneTrust panel held 24 of 27 paragraphs
  and passed itself off as the layout. That share test is right for an ambiguous
  word like *footer* and wrong for a named consent manager, which is never a
  layout class whatever its size.

**Verified 12 Sep, 20:34: 6 rows, her count, every one with genuine curatorial
text.** The 9 Displays and 4 other-site rows are each named in the log.

**Engine change re-verified in the same sitting, as her rule requires.** `ng`,
`rijks` and `acq` re-swept: identical row sets and all 79 summaries unchanged,
none blanked.

**Open for her:** dropping the Displays is read from her count, and whether a thing
is an exhibition at all is her call. It also contradicts the guide's Wallace ruling
("displays and trails kept"), so either the two venues genuinely differ or one note
