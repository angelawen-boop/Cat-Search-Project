She reviewed the scraper's output against the live sites herself. **These are her

Scope: the 12 venues marked "fixes pending" in the project guide's §2 table.
`borghese` is NOT in this review — she has not re-reviewed it since 7 Sep and the

Reading these notes: a heading marked **COUNT CORRECT** means she has verified the
number of rows the scraper produced against the live site. A blank line under a

## Her overall verdict across the 12

- **Dates and titles — generally good.**
- **Not pulling in non-exhibitions — generally good.**
- **Pagination — isolated misses on a few sites**, listed per venue below, PLUS one
  general concern (next bullet).
- **Year-archive addresses, her general concern:** where a venue changes year with a
  drop-down that changes the page address, she believed the recipes wire in 2025 and
  2024 by hand and would silently stop covering 2026, 2027 and onward.
  **Checked 12 Sep: already solved, no work needed.** No recipe contains a year.
  `expandYearArchive()` derives them at run time from the lookback floor's year up to
  last year, so the set widens on its own as time passes. Fixtures Y-001 to Y-005
  hold the line. Reported back to her.
- **Biggest issue: junk text in the summaries.** Picture captions, "join us on X for
  the opening talk", ticketing information. Two distinct harms, and the second is the
  serious one:
  1. Noise the compression model should be able to ignore.
  2. Text the model **cannot tell apart from the blurb** — an artist's name in a photo
     caption reads exactly like an artist named in the exhibition text, so compression
     can state something false with no way to know.
- **Some summaries are ticketing information and nothing else.**

### Wallace Collection — COUNT CORRECT (11); summaries all junk

| | |

Three defects here, and only the first was visible in the rows she reviewed.

1. **The bold details line.** It has no container to exclude — an ordinary
   paragraph in the same `div.rich-text` as the prose, same class, same parent,
   simply first. The one thing separating a label from a sentence is that the
   label is bold end to end, so the shape of the markup is the signal. Guarded to
   fire only where the page has prose that is not wholly bold, so a venue that
   sets its standfirst in bold keeps its description. This alone cleared 10 of 11.
2. **A PLAIN-TEXT details line** on *Winston Churchill: The Painter*, where the
   same block is not bold and the markup offers nothing. Caught on wording that
   belongs to the ticket desk and never to a description.
3. **Weekday names were breaking every date range in the parser — universal.**
   That Churchill row reached her with NO dates and a note saying the venue had
   published none. Untrue: its page says "Saturday 23 May - Sunday 29 November
   2026" in full. Strip the weekdays and the identical string parses. Any venue
   writing a weekday in front of a date was losing it the same way, and the row
   arrived looking like a badly-publishing museum. Fixtures W-001 to W-005.

**Verified 12 Sep, 20:52: 11 rows, her count, every summary clean, every row
dated.** Churchill is now 23 May - 29 Nov 2026.

### Louvre — COUNT WAS 18, TRUE COUNT IS 22; pagination and junk summaries

| | |
|---|---|

| Scraper returns | 22 — 7 + 4 + 10 + 1 |
| Her note | year pages exist for 2025, 2024 etc, but each also has a "load more" at the bottom — how does the scraper get past the first page? |
| Her note on summaries | junk like "To ensure proper maintenance and collection care, the exhibition rooms may close on wednesday" |

**The 2024 difference is correct and she confirmed it.** Its page holds 4; three
closed before the 1 July 2024 floor and are dropped by the lookback. Only
*Olympism* survives. **The 2026 closers are kept** — plainly inside the lookback,
her ruling.

**The load-more control.** The note in this recipe said clicking it did nothing.
It had never been clicked: the cookie popin sits over the page and absorbed it.
That banner is NOT shown on every visit, which is how one unlucky test became a
recorded fact for two days.

**The trap that cost 8 rows on the first live run.** The control is an anchor
whose href 404s. While there is more to load the site's own handler intercepts
the click and loads the batch in place; once the list is complete it stops
intercepting, the browser follows the href, and the listing is replaced by the
404. The failure therefore needs the press AFTER the last real one — a
single-click probe cannot show it, and mine reported success just before the live
sweep lost those rows. **A control tested once is tested in its easy case.**

The fix cancels the anchor's default action in the capture phase immediately
before each press. The site's own listener still runs, so the batch still loads;
only the browser's navigation is refused. Plus a guard: if the page moves anyway
it returns to the listing and stops.

**Clicking is right here and does not contradict the Met rule.** The test is
whether the address changes. The Met's year menu changes it, so each year was
simply another page. This loader changes nothing in the address bar and has no
address of its own that answers, so there is no page to fetch instead.

**Her count of 10 for 2025 is met, though only 8 rows say "past 2025".** That page
serves 11 links; 3 were already collected from the plain past page, and `notes`
records where a row was FOUND, not which year it belongs to.

**Visitor notices** sit in `div.Alert` on the exhibition's own page, above the
blurb. Naming the container beats listing wordings — the notice text changes week
to week, the container does not.

**Verified 12 Sep, 21:12: 22 rows, no blank summaries, no visitor notices.**
Consent dismissal and the Alert rule are engine-wide, so all ten other venues were

**Louvre is closed.** Seven venues done: Frick, Menil, V&A, Tate Modern, Tate

### Brera — COUNT CORRECT (8: 7 exhibitions + 1 marker); dates and lead-ins

- **One comma caused both date faults.** Brera writes "From May 16, 2025, to
  May 17, 2027" — a comma before the separator, which the parser did not allow.
  The range failed, the scan fell to a single date, and *Pinacoteca viaggiante* was
  recorded as **ending on its opening day**. Armani's page has the same comma,
  which is why his opening date vanished.
- **Armani needed a second fix**, and it is the more important one. The listing had
  already supplied his EXTENDED closing date, so a prose range ending 11 January
  contradicted it, and the guard that discards a contradictory range threw away the
  opening date too. The two date parsers had diverged — **the failure this project
  has already paid for twice**. Both now apply extensions.
- **Lead-in paragraphs were missing** because Brera opens each exhibition with an
  `h4`, not a paragraph, and the shared ladder only looks at paragraphs. Naming
  both, in page order, also fixed the *Oman* row, whose whole summary had been the
  "Where / When / Hours" block.

**Verified: 8 rows, her count.**

### What this review cost, and what it bought

Thirteen venues reviewed, sixteen fixed. Counts corrected at four venues — menil
20→32, va 15→6, louvre 18→22, tate-britain 10→9 — and two of those were rows lost
outright rather than filtered.

**Three times in one day a row reached her looking like a venue that publishes
badly, when the venue had published in full**: the Wallace's Churchill dates
(weekday names broke the parser), dellav's TransLucent dates (three-letter Italian
months), and Borghese's Louise Bourgeois summary (a blurb in divs, then a ticket
sentence vetoing the whole block). Each had been written down somewhere as a fact
about the site. **When a row says the venue published nothing, check the page before
believing it.**

**Two mechanisms looked like they worked and did not.** The Louvre's "load more"
had been recorded as broken when the click had never reached it, and once pressed it
still returned her original count — because the plain past page was covering for it.
A count agreeing is not proof a mechanism works, and a control tested once is tested
in its easy case.
