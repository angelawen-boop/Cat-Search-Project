{"i":81,"title":"The Imperial Crown","raw":"The Imperial Crown – an iconic symbol of the Holy Roman Empire and of European history – will be on view at the Kunsthistorisches Museum for the first time as part of a special presentation. As part of a major research initiative, scholars from across Europe joined forces in an unprecedented interdisciplinary collaboration led by the KHM. They investigated the materials used, the manufacturing techniques, and the eventful history of the Imperial Crown. Its use over many centuries, from the Middle Ages until the beginning of the nineteenth century, saved it from destruction, making it the only surviving example of the medieval royal and imperial crowns once in use. Today, it is one of the most significant surviving symbols of European history and one of the artistically and historically most valuable objects of the KHM-Museumsverband. The octagonal crown, weighing around 3.5 kilograms, is adorned with 172 precious stones and numerous pearls. Many legends surround it, such as the long-discredited myth that it was already worn by Charlemagne. The findings of the research project will be presented in a comprehensive publication in 2027. However, some of the discoveries, along with breathtaking large-scale photographs of this unique crown taken during the research, will already be featured in the special exhibition."}
